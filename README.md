# Credit Risk Analysis of PT Bank X Indonesia

## Background
Banks are financial institutions licensed to manage customers' deposits and extend loans, serving as both a secure place for customers' savings and a source for financing needs. The main role of banks is to be the meeting point for those who "have too much money" and those who need money lending. Customers who have money deposit their money into the bank, and customers who need money lend to the bank. Later on, the borrowers will pay the money back to the bank including the interest. As a reward, the bank will then share some of its profit (from the paid interest) to the depositors. The agreement between the bank and the borrowers is called Bank Loan.

When a borrower fail to pay the loan, the loan can be called as non-performing. The ratio between the value of non-performing loan and the total loan disbursed by a bank is called Non-Performing Loan Ratio, or NPL Ratio for short. NPL Ratio is used as a primary health indicator for financial institutions. A high NPL ratio means higher potential credit losses and economic stress, while a low ratio means better collections, debt restructuring, or loan write-offs.

In order to minimize NPL ratio (while still maintaining bank's profitability), it is important to have Risk Management. Risk Management is the process of identifying, assessing, and reducing potential financial losses that could affect an individual, business, or investment portfolio. One common way to assess risk is to use the Risk Assessment Matrix. The matrix usually has both 3-5 columns and rows. The rows represent likeliness, how likely the probability of the hazard occuring (from very unlikely to very likely), while the columns represent severity, how bad the impact if the risk is realized (from negligible to significant/severe). Risks in the "red zone" (high likeliness and severity) need to be mitigated immediately, since they have high possibility to create significant loss. Risks in the green zone (low likeliness and severity) are acceptable, but still need to be monitored periodically. The rest "yellow zone" risks (low likeliness and high severity, or high likeliness and low severity) are still tolerable, but need constant control and mitigation plan just in case.

In this context, Credit Risk Management process means to identify, analyze, mitigate, and monitor the potential loss from non-performing loans. There are some metrics that can be used to identify NPL risks likeliness and severity, such as Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR), Probability of Default (PD), and Expected Credit Loss (ECL). DSCR is the ratio between customer's net operating income and total debt services. The higher the ratio (usually greater than 1.0 or 1.25), the less likely the customer will be unable to pay its loan, since the income is much higher than the debt. PD, can be calculated based on DSCR, measures the probability of a customer will be in a default status (unable to pay the loan), scales between 0 and 1. The closer the PD is to 1, the more likely it will default. Most companies convert this value into an easier-to-read scale of 300 to 850, called Credit Score. The higher the score, the better. Both PD and Credit Score represent the likeliness of a Credit Risk. ECL is the expected loss when a customer failed to pay its loan.
